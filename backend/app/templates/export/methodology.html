3. Following the links automated, no AI

Every link on every captured page was extracted — {{ counts.citations }} citations in total — and sorted by rules into navigation, tag pages, social-media buttons and newsletter sign-ups (set aside), and articles, references and documents (followed). Each followed link was requested from the Wayback Machine at the capture closest to the date the site first linked to it, so that material is seen as the site’s readers would have seen it.

{{ ns.step }}. Reading the documents closely done by AI

Steps 2 and 4 sort a corpus. They do not interrogate it. The {{ readings|length }} article-shaped pages were read again, one at a time, and asked the questions put to any historical source: who wrote it, whom it quotes, whom it only writes about, where its events take place, when those events happened, what it asserts, what speech it prints, what form it takes and what it is for. Index pages, tag listings and pagination were left out — they have no author, no place and no claim, and reading them would have added noise and cost.

The model was asked only to describe what the page says and how it says it. It was never asked whether a claim is true. Verification is the historian's work, and the assertions the pages make are listed on the chronology page precisely so it can be done.

Where the questions come from

The backbone is the ordinary critical method: heurística — finding the sources — then external criticism, then internal criticism, then synthesis. Steps 1 and 3 are heuristics: deciding which period is the subject and recovering what can be recovered. External criticism is what the capture record supplies: provenance, dates, and the plain fact of what did not survive. This step is internal criticism, and the questions it asks are borrowed deliberately:

  • From sociology — the roles. A person in a text is not simply "mentioned"; they are given a byline, or quoted, or written about. Recording which lets the archive show who a publication treated as a source and who it treated as a topic. That distribution is a property of the outlet, and it is not one an outlet describes about itself. The close reading page reports it directly.
  • From literary study — genre and voice. Nota informativa, crónica, reportaje, columna, reseña are different acts, not different topics, and a publication that shifts between them has changed what it is for. Quoted speech is recorded separately from paraphrase for the same reason: who is allowed to speak in their own words is a formal choice.
  • From hermeneutics — framing. Beyond what a piece is about lies the description under which it puts its subject. Those construals are recorded in the source's own wording rather than translated into categories, so the archive preserves the terms of the discourse instead of replacing them.
  • From the discipline's own quarrel with digital sources — the two clocks. A web archive supplies exactly one date: when a crawler saw the page. Treating that as the date of the events described is the standard error. Every reading was asked separately for when the piece was published and for the period it discusses, and those are recorded as different fields.

How the counting was done

  • Names were folded for accents and case, then a shorter name was merged into a longer one only when every word of it appears in that longer name and exactly one longer name qualifies. Ambiguity was left alone: two people who share a surname stay two people. Nicknames and handles were never merged with full names.
  • A person appearing on a quarter or more of all documents is treated as part of the page furniture — a sidebar or staff box — rather than as coverage, and is listed separately as the masthead.
  • Where a URL was captured several times, one capture was read and the result attached to its duplicates. Every figure on these pages counts the originals only.

{{ ns.step }}. Recovering the audience automated, no AI

Reader comments are part of the captured page, and they are normally lost twice over: stripped as boilerplate, or absorbed into the article so that a reader arguing in a thread is counted as someone the piece is about. They were instead read straight out of the markup, which is regular enough to parse exactly — an ol.commentlist of numbered items, each with an author, a relative timestamp and a body, and a flag on the ones the site posted itself. No model was involved, so nothing here is an estimate.

Two details matter for reading the figures. Comments accumulate after publication while the article text does not, so they were taken from the last surviving capture of each page rather than the capture that was close-read, and deduplicated on the comment number the site assigned. And the timestamps are relative — "8 days ago" — so they were converted using the capture date; months are treated as 30 days, and the original wording is kept beside the derived date in comments.csv so the arithmetic can be checked or redone.

The site's own replies are identified both by the flag in the markup and by the account name, because the flag is not applied consistently: of {{ apparatus.reception.site_comments }} replies from the publication, only some carried it. Getting that wrong would have counted the editors among their own readers.

{{ ns.step }}. Drafting findings AI draft, for a person to verify

The model was given the year-by-year aggregates and a sample of page and link summaries, each with an identifier, and asked to write findings in which every claim cites those identifiers. Citations that do not match the evidence it was shown were removed; claims left without evidence are flagged as unverified.

Limits

  • The Wayback Machine did not capture everything. Absence from this archive is not evidence something did not exist.
  • A capture shows a page on the day it was crawled, which may differ from when it was written or linked.
  • AI summaries, names and tone judgements contain errors. They are finding aids that point to the evidence; they are not the evidence. Always read the capture before relying on a claim.
  • Tone (sentiment) reflects the model’s reading of each page as a whole, including quoted material and news of events the site reported on, not necessarily the authors’ own views.
    • {% if readings %}
  • Roles, places and dates were assigned by a model reading one page at a time. It has no knowledge of the region beyond the page in front of it, so an unfamiliar local name may be typed wrongly, and a person quoted in one piece and merely named in another is counted once for each.
  • An absent comment thread is not proof of silence: a page captured early shows the comments it had that day, and a page never captured after publication shows none at all.
  • The counts describe what survived to be read. Sixty per cent of the site’s own articles were never captured, so any distribution here — of people, places or forms — is a distribution over the remainder, not over what was published.
